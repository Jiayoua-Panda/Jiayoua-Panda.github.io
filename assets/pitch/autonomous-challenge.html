Part 1 · How it works
Five questions, answered in order.
1 · Where am I?
Wheels count.
The gyro turns.
The wheels measure distance. They cannot measure turning: the truck skids sideways to turn. So turning comes from the gyro, and a filter blends both 20 times a second.
1 · Where am I?
The lidar
draws the map.
360° all around · 455 points per turn · 10 turns per second
Later, every new scan is compared with this map to correct the position.
2 · Where are the docks?
Each dock has
a name tag.
To the lidar all docks look the same. The camera reads the tag: A is 0, B is 1 … H is 7.
0 / 8
docks found in simulation · each within 3 cm of its true place
3 · How do I get there?
Plan a path. Keep your distance.
Every obstacle gets a safety margin. The path stays outside it.
Something new in the way? Stop, then plan again.
4 · How do I park?
Stop. Look. Drive in.
The front bumper stops about 9 cm from the wall. The rule asks for 14 cm or less.
5 · What next?
Ask. Drive.
Wait. Repeat.
Saga AI is Einride's delivery planner. The truck asks it once a second where to go, and never tells it what to do.

Built for the real truck · lessons 2 to 8
Seven more surprises.
Centimetres, not metres.
The wheel counter was 100× off. The truck thought it stood still.
The clock jumped.
Camera time was 17 minutes behind. No dock was ever saved.
The gyro drifts.
False turning in about 8 minutes: 175° → 15°
Turns at one third.
The wheels skid: it turned at 0.31 of the asked speed. Now it measures that and adapts.
Close-ups lie.
Very close, a tag gives a wrong direction. So: no learning up close, and saved docks are frozen when the mission starts.
Boxed in.
Nose against an obstacle, it could not turn or reverse. Now the lidar checks behind and it backs out 25 cm.
A small computer.
The Jetson is busy. The camera sends 10 pictures a second, and the obstacle map is coarser.
None of these show up in simulation. Each one is now a check or a fix in the code.
